# End-of-session success with short and regular explanations

## Overview

This descriptive study compares end-of-session success across two explanation versions in 20 sessions recorded at a single facility during the same week. Participants chose either a short explanation, labelled S, or the regular explanation, labelled R. Ten sessions used each version. The question is how the recorded success fractions differed between these two groups within that week. The sessions were not randomly assigned to versions, so the comparison describes the records rather than identifying an effect of explanation length.

Success meant answering all four questions correctly at the end of a session. Seven sessions using S met this criterion, compared with four using R. The difference is 30 percentage points. This result concerns the recorded sessions and the specified end-of-session criterion; it does not establish whether changing the explanation would improve a participant's performance or whether the difference would recur at other facilities or times.

## Method

Each session contributed one record. The 20 records contained no duplicates or missing values, and no sessions were excluded from the comparison. The observation unit was the session, rather than an independently established sample of distinct people. Both groups used the same success definition: all four answers correct counted as success, and every other outcome counted as non-success. This common criterion permits the two fractions to summarize the same recorded outcome. It does not establish that the groups had similar knowledge before their sessions.

We use the grouped completion contrast to compare these session outcomes. For each group, the success fraction is its number of successful sessions divided by its number of recorded sessions. With S as the selected group and R as the reference group, the contrast in percentage points is \(100[(\text{S successes}/\text{S sessions})-(\text{R successes}/\text{R sessions})]\). The method requires comparable definitions of success and one record per session, both satisfied here. It yields a descriptive difference between the recorded groups; it supplies neither random assignment nor adjustment for differences between participants. The name, definition, requirements, and formula are adopted from M, §1, Definition A.

Participants selected their explanation version themselves. Their reasons for choosing a version and their prior knowledge were not measured. These unmeasured features could differ between groups and could relate to the recorded outcome. Consequently, applying the contrast does not separate explanation length from other possible reasons for the difference. No subsequent follow-up was recorded, so the analysis also cannot describe retention or later performance.

## Findings

The success fraction was 7/10, or 70%, for S and 4/10, or 40%, for R. The remaining records comprised three non-successes for S and six for R. Applying the stated contrast gives \(100(0.70-0.40)=30\) percentage points: the success fraction was higher among sessions using S in these records. This is an absolute difference between fractions. The 30-point contrast summarizes immediate outcomes; it is not a measure of improvement caused by shortening the explanation.

The supported finding is limited to this facility's 20 recorded sessions during the same week. The contrast provides no conclusion about statistical significance, no causal estimate, and no generalization to learners as a population. It identifies a difference in immediate recorded success that a separate study could investigate, while leaving its explanation and persistence unresolved.
